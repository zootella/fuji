# Tabs

Fuji will have tabs in two places, the video window and the main window, and they are two different kinds.


## The video window: a tabset of toolbars

**The video window's controls are a palette of tabs, just like a Microsoft Office ribbon.** Each tab brings forward a varied arrangement of toolbar buttons, so the palette is a tabset of toolbars. It holds the media controls, like playback.

**The palette goes beneath the video, or to the side of a vertical video.**

**The media controls roll out on the video window**, which is itself entirely a test bench today.


## The main window: tabs like a web browser's

**The main window's tabs are like tabs in a web browser**, letting the user switch between any number of sets of the main components of the window beneath them: the tree, the list, and above them a location bar, which does not exist yet.

**The tab strip is probably not at the very top of the window.**


## No ribbon in the main window

**The main window's toolbar intentionally does not gain tabs of toolbars as the Microsoft ribbon does**, a design the user finds awful for an application. Tabs of toolbars are the plan for the video window's palette alone.


## Open

**Whether the tabs are a control of their own or a part of FujiBar.** The first thought was a control of its own, and it is not settled.

**Whether the video window's toolbars are FujiBar toolbars at all.** They may be custom enough to be Vue controls of their own, like the panel on the bench, with FujiBar buttons used within them, rather than being FujiBar toolbars themselves.
