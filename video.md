# Video

Fuji will play video through libmpv. Decided 2026-09-29 on the Windows box after a research pass. What is built lives in the code's essays: the library carried by all six packages, the four player commands, and a video playing in the audio panel's region on Windows and on the Mac, built for Linux and not yet seen on a Linux desktop. This document owns playback — how a video file reaches the screen and the speakers once fuji has decided to show it. What fuji claims to open is `fileTypes.js`, whose video and audio entries are on and carry the `mpv` render tag.

## What is decided

**libmpv, through Rust.** Three reasons, in the order they weighed. It is FFmpeg underneath, so it plays what VLC plays, which is everything — and for fuji that word means the video a 1990s collection actually holds, which is not H.264 in an MP4. It is AVI with Cinepak, Indeo or Microsoft Video 1 inside, MPEG-1 off a Video CD, QuickTime with Sorenson, RealVideo, and later DivX, Windows Media and Flash Video; `fileTypes.js` lists them all, and every one is on. No webview plays any of that, and neither does QuickTime Player or Movies & TV, so libmpv is the only route that makes such a folder open at all, the way the picture entries already open a folder of Paint's BMPs. Its render API draws into a context the host owns, instead of demanding a window of its own, and mpv's own documentation now recommends that route over window embedding, especially on macOS. Its video output is the best in open source — libplacebo's `gpu-next` is the default since 0.41, with color management, HDR tone mapping and proper scalers — and that is the part of playback fuji cares about, since fuji measures whether a picture's colors arrive intact.

**Why not the others**, written once so nobody researches it twice. *libVLC*: VLC 4 was still beta and nightly on the desktop as of mid-2026 and libVLC 4 pre-release with it; the API is from the 2001 module design, and people embedding it in Tauri report it rendering over the webview rather than under it. *FFmpeg directly*: the right tool when frames are data — thumbnails, scrubbing — and it may still come in for that, but as a player it means writing audio output and A/V sync ourselves. *GStreamer*: a framework rather than a player, heavy to ship, and Windows and the Mac are its second-class platforms; it is what WebKitGTK already plays through. *The webview's video tag*: free, and kept in the table as the engine tags, `webkit`, `edge` and `gtk`, on every video an engine plays, and the section on the two routes below says what it is for. *AVFoundation and Media Foundation*: three implementations to maintain, each playing only what its operating system plays.

**Nothing is drawn over a video.** No controls, no title, no readout, nothing that appears because the mouse moved; the Design Choices page on fuji's site says why. That is a design rule, and it also settles where libmpv's pixels go: into a native view on top of the webview, opaque, so the webview is never made transparent and none of the costs transparency carries on each platform, the Mac's private-API feature among them, is paid. Two things happen on the video, and neither draws: a click anywhere on it, at any time, toggles play and pause, and a drag on it moves the whole window. And a video's window is sized to the video: Fuji Video never crops, letterboxes or stretches.

**No cover art, and nothing loaded from beside the file.** Fuji never shows a song's cover art; that is a music player's habit of 2010, and not why fuji plays sound. mpv looks for cover art and for subtitle files of the same name on every load by default, by reading the file's whole folder, since `cover-art-auto` and `sub-auto` both default to `exact` in mpv 0.41.0, read in its `options/options.c`; `autoload-files=no` turns all of it off. Its `ordered-chapters`, on by default, also loads segments from other files in the folder, and `access-references=no` turns off references to other files, which mpv's manual calls helpful when a file is scanned automatically. Which of these the player is started with, and `embeddedfonts=no` against a Matroska file's font attachments, are open.

## Bigger than the screen (nicknamed "BigVideoWindow")

A video larger than the screen, shown at one of its pixels per panel pixel, the way a phone's video would be on a Retina Mac: a vertical 4K video, 2160 by 3840, on a standard-DPI screen 1200 tall, with the top sitting above the desktop while the user watches the lower part, panning by dragging the window the way the light table pans a picture. QuickTime, VLC and the others never show such a video's real raster pixels without a screen bigger than the video. The player needs nothing new for it, since mpv draws at any size and the native view can be any size. The drag that moves the window from anywhere on the video carries it off any of the four edges of the screen, the top included, so the user brings the part they want into view: seen on the Mac mini, and on Windows 10 by the same command, where a window left past the top comes back to the same place after a minimize and after the monitor sleeps. What is left to build is the window's size: it grows past the screen to the video's own. Linux does not offer any of it: Wayland lets no application place its own window or run its own drag, which keeps a hostile one from making windows dance under the pointer, and GNOME and KDE hold a window's top below their top bar during their own drag.

What is known about a window bigger than the screen, which nothing has tried yet:

- **On the Mac**, the override that lets the video window past the menu bar also lifts AppKit's cut of a window with a title bar to the screen's height. `maxSize` defaults to the largest float, and the limits Apple documents, 10,000 for a window's size and ±16,000 for its position, were not enforced in a probe on the Mac mini. Its M2 caps a GL texture and viewport at 16384 backing pixels, under which fall a 2160 by 3840 video at either scale and 8K at one video pixel per CSS pixel on Retina, 15360 by 8640.
- **On the Mac with several displays**, Displays have separate Spaces is on by default, and then "windows may not visibly span displays… A window clips to the edge of the display", so a window bigger than one screen shows only on its own display.
- **On Windows the size is already free, by the code.** Microsoft says DefWindowProc validates a window's size as `WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGING` passes through it, and that "by not passing the WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGING message to the DefWindowProc function, an application can override these defaults" ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winmsg/wm-windowposchanging)). tao answers that message itself and never passes it on. A resize by the border past the screen's size takes a maximum size set through Tauri, which tao writes into `ptMaxTrackSize`, and a value too big for a 32-bit integer turns negative there. Direct3D 11 caps a texture at 16384.
- **A window covering the whole monitor on Windows is taken for fullscreen**, and covers the taskbar, unless it carries the window property `NonRudeHWND` before it is shown, per [Raymond Chen](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20250522-00/?p=111211).
- **A move to a monitor at another scale grows a window by that scale** on Windows 11, which tao applies as it is, and `WM_GETDPISCALEDSIZE` lets a window ask to keep its size in backing pixels instead.
- **The pointer cannot leave the screen**, so a window goes past an edge only by the distance from where it was grabbed to that edge: a window taller than the screen, grabbed near its bottom, can put most of itself above the top.

That window is a native window of its own, decided on 2026-10-21: a video window stands beside the contact sheet rather than taking its place, there can be several, and it shares none of a browsing window's state, so it is made for the video alone.

## Two routes, and the manifest

`fileTypes.js` records two routes for a video, the way it records two for a picture: the webview's own video tag, by the engine's render tag, `webkit`, `edge` or `gtk`, one per platform, and libmpv, by `mpv`. The first argument between them is capability. The webview plays only what its engine plays: WebView2 plays H.264 out of the box and HEVC only once the HEVC Video Extensions package from the Store is installed and licensed; WebKit on the Mac plays what QuickTime plays; WebKitGTK plays through GStreamer, only from `http(s)`, `file` and `blob` URLs — a custom scheme fails before a pipeline is built — and only with the plugins the machine has. libmpv plays everything in the table. Both record capability: an engine's tag goes on a kind that engine plays on fuji's floor, with the reason in the entry's comment, and no web player exists yet, so every video takes the mpv road, which `fileTypesRoads` says; where both could play, the measurement below decides which the page prefers.

The second argument is performance, and it cuts the other way. On the Mac, a recent, long, huge 4K file visibly costs VLC something: by eye, around a hundred milliseconds of spinning up before the first frame, and a seek that is slow. The very same file dragged into Chrome plays as if it took no effort, and seeks the same way. So the route is not simply the web for the small, the old and the odd, and libmpv for the new. The guess is that the engine's player is tuned for exactly the file a phone or a video site makes today, with the platform's hardware decoder underneath, and whether libmpv matches it on that file is not known: libmpv is not VLC, and mpv reaches the same hardware decoders, so the gap may close or may not, and measurement decides.

To explore, once libmpv plays at all: the same big modern file through both routes on each machine, with libmpv's hardware decoding on, and time to first frame, time to seek and frames dropped written to the log. Then the results decide which of the two the page prefers where both can play, as `fileTypesSheet` and `fileTypesTable` decide for pictures; the tags stay what each road can do.

## Shipping libmpv

The recipe leaves mpv's gpl option at its default, on, and FFmpeg's off. The files mpv's option adds are outputs and disc inputs, Direct3D 9 on Windows and the X11 outputs on Linux among them, and no decoder or demuxer, so it changes nothing about what plays.

## The binding and the commands

**mpv's events, forwarded up as Tauri events,** are still to come. A load answers as soon as mpv has queued it, so knowing when a file has loaded or failed takes mpv's events, `start-file` and then `file-loaded` or `end-file`, which the two questions in `media.rs` wait on and the panel's play does not yet.

## Beside playback

- **A double-click on the sheet.** Once the contact sheet lists videos, a double-click on one opens it in a video window, through `window_build` like every other way to one; until then v asks for an empty one.
- **A frame for the sheet.** A video's thumbnail. The operating system's thumbnailer can make a frame of a video on Windows and the Mac, which `thumbnail.rs` does not ask for. Linux has no native route, and `media_frame` serves it, a frame from any video the library plays, already turned and with its pixels made square, and may serve all three if it is as fast. At what point in the file is a thumbnail question, open now that video is switched on.

## What could break, and has not yet

Concerns on the floor that nobody has met, kept here because the subject is playback and each is a year or more out. The toolchains are the unpinned part of the library's recipe: MSYS2 rolls, Homebrew's meson and ninja float, and Apple's Swift is whatever Xcode the Mac has, and mpv's Swift bridge is the sharpest edge, since mpv tracks Swift closely and a future Swift could refuse to build 0.41's, at which point the fix is bumping mpv, which may bump what it needs beneath; the recipe records the tools in every library's record, so a drift is diagnosable. OpenGL on the Mac is deprecated since 2018 and still shipped, and it is the one hardware backend mpv's render API has; every mpv-based Mac application shares this, and the fallback if Apple removes it is the render API's software renderer or a Vulkan backend over MoltenVK, neither built today. The three native rectangles in `window.rs` each lean on how wry arranges its views, a sibling above the WKWebView on the Mac, a child raised above WebView2's on Windows, the WebKit view lifted into an overlay on Linux, and a wry release that rearranges any of them breaks placement or drops rather than the library, with the pinned Tauri in the lockfile as the guard. And a pinned release receives no security fix without a bump, which is one hash line per package in the recipe.
