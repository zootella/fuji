# Video spike

libmpv in fuji, on the Mac, Windows and Linux: one library built by one recipe from pinned sources and carried by all six packages; four player commands and a panel that are the same code on every platform; a native rectangle a video draws in, one per platform by necessity; and a video playing in it, seen on Windows and on the Mac, and built for Linux, where it waits on its first smoke test. The upper levels of fuji build on this floor, Fuji Video, Fuji Audio, and the walk that reads a collection's files overnight, and the test of the floor is that none of them has to come back down here to muck with builds, tags, libraries or dlls. This document is the floor described and critiqued: what is built, what in it would break in a year, what differs per platform, where the bytes come from, and what remains.

## What is built

**The library.** `desktop/libmpv/build.py` fetches fifteen pinned sources, ten on every platform and five more on Windows for Direct3D's shaders, each a release tarball checked against the sha256 written in the recipe or a clone checked against its commit, and builds them static in order into one shared library with nothing in it from anywhere else: mpv 0.41.0 on FFmpeg 9.0.2, with libplacebo, dav1d, libass and its font stack, lcms2 and zlib inside. The toolchain is the package manager's and nothing else is, Homebrew's meson, ninja and pkgconf over Apple's clang and Swift on the Mac, MSYS2's ucrt64 on Windows, Debian 12's in the containers with meson from PyPI, and the library's imports are the proof: Windows' own libraries and the universal C runtime on Windows, Apple's frameworks on the Mac, the distribution's fontconfig, X11, OpenGL and two sound libraries on Linux, which the deb and the rpm depend on by name. Every package built once is skipped on the next run, under a stamp of the function that builds it, the flags it is given and the stamp of the package before it, so a changed flag rebuilds that package and everything after it, and a rebuild starts from a clean tree. Beside the library the recipe writes `libmpv.json`: the library's hash and size, the day, the hash of the recipe, the tools, and every source with its hash, which `pnpm hash` copies into the release's sidecar, refusing a library the recipe as checked out did not build, a library in `binaries/` that is not the one its record describes, or an installer built before the library was.

The dll measured on the Windows box on 2026-10-12, the rest on the Mac mini on 2026-10-15, the Linux libraries in the containers there: the dylib hashed `622f89438d2fca1453b34b3f6234dcbea85e3d6e2165078f97e7b3df660085ed`, the arm64 library `7e8cb6847268ae66c582dbb62f8124ae87c781efc01cc545088d21178a2215af` and the x86-64 one `a93987f6fc73f4ba9456ff36ff052bd7318654472b38015e739a0a1e1c936642`, each written in its record beside the library. A clean build takes about three minutes on the Mac mini, FFmpeg 72 seconds of it, about twenty on the Windows box, FFmpeg nine and shaderc four, and in the containers three minutes on arm64 and twelve on the emulated amd64, which puts the Linux run at twenty-two minutes against six before. Each platform carries its library its own way, and each way is a tracked configuration file Tauri merges on that platform alone: a resource beside the executable on Windows, a framework in the bundle on the Mac, which `build.rs` links against and the rpath finds, and a resource under the product's folder on Linux, found by the rpath `build.rs` gives the binary, with the flatpak laying the same folder out under its own root.

**What it costs at launch**, measured the way the shell's first two log lines measure every launch, warm, from the operating system starting the process to the first frame, and the host's footprint two seconds later. The Mac mini, 2026-10-13: 400 to 420 ms to the first frame against 404 to 413 before the library, 28 to 40 ms to main against 30 to 42, the host at rest about 24 MB against 22. The Windows box, 2026-10-12: 471 to 480 ms against 500, 19 to 22 ms to main against 29, the host at rest 4.3 to 4.6 MB against 4.2, and the cold launch after an install 557 to 570 ms against 664. So a library mapped at launch costs nothing a collector could notice on either machine, and the web engine's processes never moved. The Mac's notarized release is two minutes end to end with the library in it, measured on 2026-10-12 against the day before: five seconds more to sign, nine more to upload twice, and Apple's own check unchanged.

**The binding** is thirteen declarations written by hand in `mpv.rs` against `client.h` and `render.h`, nine of the client API and four of the render API, with no crate and no bindgen. The client API's second major version has held its signatures since 2018, and the functions fuji calls are the handful whose shape every embedding uses.

**The four commands and the panel** are the same code on every platform. A player is started for the window with the options the page chooses, sent one mpv command with its arguments, read one property as text, and stopped; each is generic over mpv's own vocabulary, so a seek, a frame step or a still is a send and never a command of its own. One player per window by its label, every call into the library under that player's lock, and a window that closes mid-play takes its player with it on the blocking pool. The panel owns the rest: what plays, when, the click that toggles pause, the drag that moves the window, the double click into fuji's fullscreen and back, and the position read every half second, which is the one road back from the player until events come up. On the Windows box the player started in 7 ms and `loadfile` answered in 2, Big Buck Bunny played through Direct3D 11 with hardware decoding and no frame dropped, and the host at rest went from 4.5 MB idle to about 57 with the film playing.

**The rectangle is per platform, by necessity, and it is fenced in `window.rs`.** mpv takes a window to draw in on X11, Windows and Android and nowhere else, so Windows hands it one and the other two draw through the render API.

- Windows: a child window of fuji's own above WebView2's, handed to mpv as `wid`, which makes a window of its own inside it and presents through Direct3D 11. mpv disables that window when embedded, so the mouse over the video reaches fuji's window around it and goes up to the page as the event `video-mouse`; a drop target of the child's own, registered the way wry registers the web view's, sends a dropped file up as `video-drop`. About 120 lines, and three roads not to walk again are written in its essay.
- The Mac: a view of fuji's own above the WKWebView, hosting a CAOpenGLLayer with a GL context made beside it, the arrangement mpv's own Mac window is built on. The view answers nil to hit tests, so the page's own handlers see the mouse and the web view takes the drop. mpv.rs makes a render context against that GL context, Core Animation asks the layer to draw on the main thread, and the frame is announced at its display time rather than fifty milliseconds ahead, so a render never blocks the page's events. About 200 lines, and the smoke test passed by hand on 2026-10-14: the drop, the click, the drag, a second file mid-play, the double click into fullscreen and back, with sound.
- Linux: a GTK GL area in an overlay over the WebKit view, since every Linux target runs Wayland, where mpv cannot be handed a window. The area is pass-through, GTK's own word for the Mac's nil hit test, so the page sees the mouse and the drop. GTK's GL context is current on the main thread alone, so a render API call from the pool takes its turn there with the pool waiting, and the area's unrealize frees the render context while there is still a context to be current. The C numeric locale goes back to C after GTK sets it from the environment, or `mpv_create` answers null. About 170 lines, the render API code shared with the Mac, built on 2026-10-15 and compiled and linked in the containers, and not yet run on a screen.

## Critique

**Brittle, in six to eighteen months?** Mostly not. Every source is pinned by hash and fails loudly if it moves, and the library's own API is the stable one. The exposures, in order of likelihood:

- The toolchains are the unpinned part. MSYS2 is a rolling distribution, Homebrew's meson and ninja float, and Apple's Swift is whatever Xcode the Mac has; mpv's Swift bridge is the sharpest edge, since mpv tracks Swift closely and a future Swift could refuse to build 0.41's, at which point the fix is bumping mpv, which may bump what it needs beneath. The recipe records the tools in the terminal and in the library's record, which makes a drift diagnosable rather than impossible.
- OpenGL on the Mac is deprecated since 2018 and still shipped, and it is the one hardware backend the render API has. Every mpv-based Mac application shares this. The fallback if Apple removes it is the render API's software renderer or a Vulkan backend over MoltenVK, neither of which is built today.
- wry's view trees. The Mac view is added as a sibling above the WKWebView in its superview; the Windows child window raises itself above WebView2's and registers its own drop target because wry registered only the children that existed at creation; the Linux view lifts the WebKit view out of wry's box into an overlay. A wry release that rearranges any of the three breaks placement or drops, not the library, and the pinned Tauri in the lockfile is the guard.
- Tauri runs a plain command on the main thread, which the three views rely on and check at runtime, so a change there would be an error rather than a crash.
- A pinned release receives no security fix without a bump, and a bump is one hash line per package.

**Bespoke per platform?** The page is one codebase, every line. `mpv.rs` is one codebase, with about ninety lines of render API that the Mac and Linux share and Windows does not compile. The bespoke part is the native rectangle, three mechanisms in `window.rs` because the platforms offer three, and the mouse differs the same way, forwarded as events on Windows and seen directly on the other two. Linux is the Mac minus Cocoa rather than a third design.

**Provenance, simple and trustworthy?** Yes for the sources: fifteen pinned by hash in a tracked script, built the same way on three platforms, with the versions the About section shows. The library is reproducible: on the Mac mini on 2026-10-15 two builds from nothing but the tarballs gave every static library identical, and a build and two relinks under full load gave one dylib, `622f89438d2fca1453b34b3f6234dcbea85e3d6e2165078f97e7b3df660085ed`, once the Swift compiler was kept from writing a per-run hash into its objects and local symbols were kept out of the link, since Apple's linker writes them in whichever order its threads finish and folds the file into the dylib's UUID before they are stripped; and on the Windows box on 2026-10-12 a build and two relinks gave the same dll three times. The record beside the library and the sidecar that carries it close the gap that was left: a library built from stale packages shipped in the dmg of 2026-10-14, since a marker said a package was built when its flags had moved after, and nothing compared the library to the recipe. The stamps answer the first and the recipe's hash in the record the second. What stays unpinned is the toolchain, recorded rather than pinned, and the Windows box has not run the recipe under the stamps yet, so its dll's hash may move once, for the reason the Mac's did.

**Ready for the file questions?** In shape, and two pieces short. The running time, the width and the height are a `loadfile` on a player started with `vid=no`, paused, and three reads; mpv keeps a current frame under its null output with no window at all, and `screenshot-raw` answers it in memory as width, height, stride and bytes, which crosses to the page as a thumbnail does, after a `seek` to the time asked. The two pieces: a declaration of `mpv_command_node` and the forty lines that read a node, which the still needs, or `screenshot-to-file` with a file round trip and no new declaration; and a way to wait, since a load and a seek finish on mpv's own time and the floor only polls, which is `mpv_wait_event` or a command that waits for a property. A scanner also wants a player that is not the window's, since today one player belongs to each window and starting another replaces it; keying players by a name the page chooses, with the window's label as the default, is a small change to `mpv.rs` and none to the page.

## What remains

- **The Mac's smoke test** of the installer in the bundle folder, which carries the library the recipe builds from nothing; the one seen playing on 2026-10-14 carried the stale build.
- **Two wrinkles seen on the Mac on 2026-10-15, both the page's, and neither the library's nor the pipeline's.** A vertical video in the square shows mpv's own bars left and right, since the square is a fixed 640 by 360 and the player is started with `keepaspect`, and a narrow light gray gap below the video, which reads as the square's own background where the native view stops a fraction short of the box, since the view is placed from the box's rectangle rounded to whole backing pixels, unmeasured. Fuji Video sizes its window to the video and never letterboxes, which is the product's sprint, and the gap is the whole-pixel rule the canvases already follow applied to the square. And a double click shows a flicker of play and pause before the screen changes, since its two clicks each toggle pause at once, which is the price of a single click acting at once; the choice between that, a single click delayed by the double-click interval, and another gesture for the screen is the page's. mpv's aspect, zoom and background options exist in every build, and the rectangle and the mouse never reach the recipe.
- **The file questions**, above.
- **The extension cluster.** Switch on the video entries in `fileTypes.js` that libmpv plays, so the settings panel shows them as chips under whichever program opens each one today, and give `Info.plist` and the Windows uninstaller's list the same entries; whether the audio entries come on in the same pass. What a double-click on an enabled video file does while nothing draws video, and what the sheet shows for one, are the product's questions.
- **A smoke script.** The Windows session's script, which started the binary on the film, clicked the square, dragged it, closed the window and read the log, found every obstacle there without anyone's eyes; whether a smoke like it belongs in the repository, and under what name, is open.
