# Three video window spikes

## The order

- **Next, the Mac's edges: the override on `TaoWindow`, and a drag up past the menu bar,** which says whether WindowServer stops the Mac's own drag by itself.
- **Then Windows: a window bigger than the screen, and a drag fuji runs itself past the top,** written on the Mac and walked on the Windows box.
- **Linux's edges are what its desktop allows.**

## The buttons and the corners, still open

- **None of it has run on Windows or Linux.** On Windows, tauri-runtime-wry 2.11.4 lays a child window of its own, `TAURI_DRAG_RESIZE_WINDOW`, across the top of an undecorated window with a shadow, `SM_CYFRAME` deep, answering as the top resize border (`undecorated_resizing.rs`), so the top few backing pixels of fuji's buttons may show the resize arrow and never light. Tauri keeps that shadow on a window with no title bar, and the shadow keeps Windows' resize border, so all four edges resize, read in tao 0.35.3's `WM_NCHITTEST` and not seen. Believed and not seen: Windows 11 rounds the corners of such a window, and Windows 10 leaves them square. On Linux, tao shows a resize arrow within 5 pixels of any edge and starts the window manager's resize on a press there, and its own comment says the arrow goes back to the default once the resize begins (tao 0.35.3's Linux event loop); fuji's close button sits flush in that corner. Believed and not seen, the window has square corners and no shadow.
- **Fuji's own buttons show on the Mac too, beside the traffic lights, until they are turned off there.**
- **The video's native view in this window will land a title bar too low on the Mac.** `video_frame` in window.rs measures the page from the window's content layout rectangle, which leaves out a title bar even when the bar is transparent, while WebKit insets the page under a title bar only when the bar is not transparent (`PageClientImplMac.mm`). So the view needs measuring from the content view once a video plays in this window. Read from the code, not seen.

## Above the top of the screen, and bigger than it

- **The want.** On the Mac, Windows and Linux, the user drags the video window anywhere, above the menu bar and past every edge, and the window can be taller, wider, or bigger in both than the screen, with no window manager correcting it or moving it back.
- **Why.** Many videos have a raster taller than the screen, or bigger in both directions. A vertical 4K video downloaded from TikTok or the like is 2160 by 3840, and a collector may want it at one raster pixel per panel pixel on a standard-DPI screen 1200 pixels tall, watching the lower part with the top sitting harmlessly above the desktop. Or the screen is 1024 by 768 and the video is 4K in slow motion, at one raster pixel per CSS pixel per backing pixel per panel pixel all the way through. The user reviews it by dragging the window to pan from one part to another, the way the light table pans a picture, but in a window rather than fullscreen, with the menu bar, the dock and the taskbar still there. Every pixel of the video reaches the window even when the window does not all reach the screen. QuickTime, VLC and the others never show such a video's real raster pixels without a screen bigger than the video.
- **The questions.** On each platform, whether this is possible and how hard: how a window gets above the top, taller than the screen, and bigger than it; what fuji has to ask for or turn off to allow it; and how to keep the operating system from taking fright and pulling the window back on screen or cutting it to size.
- **Rolls up at the top, the user's idea.** Where a platform stops the window at the top of the screen, or at the menu bar, a drag that keeps going up shortens the window from the bottom while the page slides its contents up inside it: first the margin goes, then the top of the video, until the video is entirely past the window's top, and a drag down brings it all back. The window never leaves the screen, so no platform has anything to correct, and mpv could draw only the part of the video that shows. It needs a drag fuji runs itself, since while a platform runs its own drag the page does not see the pointer, and it resizes the window on every move with the page shifting its contents in step, which can let the two land a frame apart.
- **The drag fuji uses today, and why from anywhere.** The page starts the platform's own window drag once the pointer has moved 4 CSS pixels with the button down: on the Mac tao calls AppKit's `performWindowDragWithEvent:`, on Windows it posts the window `WM_NCLBUTTONDOWN` with `HTCAPTION` so Windows runs its own move loop, Snap included, and on Linux it asks GTK to `begin_move_drag`. The wait is because on Windows the release never reaches the page once the move loop begins, which the audio panel found, so a drag begun on the press would swallow every double-click. The drag starts from anywhere because a window whose top had gone past an edge would otherwise have nothing left to grab, and a video window lost past an edge closes on v while it has the focus.
- **The pointer cannot leave the screen**, on every platform and for any drag, so a window goes past an edge only by the distance from where it was grabbed to that edge: an 800 by 600 window grabbed by its middle can put 300 pixels above the top, and a window taller than the screen, grabbed near its bottom, most of itself. Snap on Windows and the Mac's tiling act when the pointer itself touches an edge.
- **On the Mac, AppKit's own drag stops the video window at the menu bar,** seen on the Mac mini on 2026-10-21 while the window was borderless; a window with a title bar is held there by the same rule.
- **On the Mac, one method keeps both kinds of window below the menu bar, and an application may override it.** Apple's AppKit notes for 10.9: "windows are now constrained to not intersect the menu bar on their containing space. This restriction was already in place for titled windows, but it has been extended to borderless windows whose level is at least NSNormalWindowLevel but less than NSMainMenuWindowLevel. This behavior is implemented in -[NSWindow constraintFrameRect:toScreen:]. You may override that method in an NSWindow subclass to adjust or prevent this constraining." For a titled resizable window the same method also cuts the height to the screen's, per [Apple's documentation of `constrainFrameRect(_:to:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nswindow/constrainframerect(_:to:)).
- **Measured on the Mac mini with Sequoia,** with invisible windows a research session made on 2026-10-21. A borderless window at the normal level placed with its top above the menu bar lands with its top at the menu bar's bottom, and placing it hidden only puts that off to the reveal. A window taller than the screen is left where it is once it covers the screen's whole height, so a window 3840 tall can already sit with its top far above the screen. A titled resizable window is cut to the screen's visible height and pulled back on screen when placed far off. With `constrainFrameRect:toScreen:` answering the frame unchanged, a window placed above the menu bar stayed there, through hiding and showing again.
- **tao's window class has no such method, so fuji can add one.** The runtime's `class_addMethod` adds it to `TaoWindow`, which worked on a live window in that measurement. It covers every window of the class, so it decides per window and calls NSWindow's own for the rest. Swapping one window's class instead is ruled out: tao's `sendEvent:` finds its superclass from the window at runtime, and WebKit's key-value observing has already swapped the class. An override that never constrains also turns off AppKit's rescue of a window whose display goes away ([cmux #7084](https://github.com/manaflow-ai/cmux/pull/7084)); Apple's FullScreenWindow sample suspends constraining only while a flag is set.
- **Whether WindowServer stops the Mac's own drag at the menu bar by itself is not known.** AppKit hands the drag to WindowServer and corrects where it ends through `constrainFrameRect:toScreen:`, read in a disassembly of AppKit on Sequoia. One drag with the override in place settles it.
- **Electron and mpv override the same method.** Electron's `enableLargerThanScreen` answers the frame unchanged for a window with no visible title bar, "put the window wherever the developer wanted it to go", and keeps the position constrained for one with a title bar (`electron_ns_window.mm`). mpv's lets its window be any height and keeps the top below the menu bar.
- **A drag fuji runs itself on the Mac.** The page keeps getting the mouse while the button is down outside the window, since dragged events go to the view that took the press. WebKit works out `screenX` from where the window is at that moment, so a window moving under it can feed its own move back. Chromium's tab drag runs in the application instead: a local event monitor, `[NSEvent mouseLocation]`, and `setFrame:display:NO animate:NO` (`window_move_loop.mm`). A move the application makes skips what WindowServer's drag gives, Space switching and tiling among them, and tao's `set_position` always lands a turn later on the main queue.
- **Sequoia tiles a window dragged to an edge or a corner.** Sources disagree on whether that is on by default or needs Option held: [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2024/11/08/how-to-tame-sequoias-window-tiling/) says on, [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/06/11/tiled-window-management-comes-to-macos-sequoia-but-its-hidden) says Option, and one developer found no per-window way out.
- **Size on the Mac.** `maxSize` defaults to the largest float. Apple documents limits of 10,000 for a window's size and ±16,000 for its position, and neither was enforced in the measurement. The Mac mini's M2 caps a GL texture and viewport at 16384 backing pixels, under which fall a 2160 by 3840 window at either scale and 8K at one video pixel per CSS pixel on Retina, 15360 by 8640.
- **Several displays on the Mac.** With Displays have separate Spaces on, the default, Apple says "windows may not visibly span displays… A window clips to the edge of the display", so a window bigger than one screen shows only on its own display.
- **On Windows, Windows' own drag keeps the top on the screen.** Users report the title bar snapping back below the top on Windows 10 ([2017](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/2795812/i-actually-want-to-move-a-window-offscreen)), and since Windows 11 24H2, build 26100.2033, a drag that stops where the whole title bar shows ([a Windows 11 user](https://www.elevenforum.com/t/title-bar-can-no-longer-be-moved-off-screen.23050/)). mpv's `w32_common.c` says "Windows itself applies the same constraint during manual move", while "it's not rejected at the Windows API". Whether it holds for a drag begun in a window's client area, as fuji's is, is not yet seen.
- **A window fuji places itself on Windows goes anywhere.** SetWindowPos puts it where it is told, and it skips Snap, which comes with the system's drag.
- **Size on Windows is already free, by the code.** Microsoft: "For a window with the WS_OVERLAPPED or WS_THICKFRAME style, the DefWindowProc function sends the WM_GETMINMAXINFO message to the window. This is done to validate the new size and position", and "By not passing the WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGING message to the DefWindowProc function, an application can override these defaults" ([WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGING](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winmsg/wm-windowposchanging)). tao answers that message itself and never passes it on, so `window_frame_set` should make a window bigger than the screen as it stands. A border drag past the screen's size takes a maximum size set through Tauri, which tao writes into `ptMaxTrackSize`, since [Microsoft documents](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/nf-winuser-getsystemmetrics) that "the user cannot drag the window frame to a size larger than" `SM_CXMAXTRACK` and `SM_CYMAXTRACK`, which refer "to the entire desktop", and a value too big for a 32-bit integer turns negative there. Direct3D 11 caps a texture at 16384. None of it has run.
- **A window covering the whole monitor on Windows is taken for fullscreen**, and covers the taskbar, unless it carries the window property `NonRudeHWND` before it is shown, per [Raymond Chen in 2025](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20250522-00/?p=111211). A window covering less goes under the taskbar.
- **A move to a monitor at another scale grows the window by that scale.** Windows 11 hands over a rectangle scaled for the new DPI, which tao applies as it is, and `WM_GETDPISCALEDSIZE` lets a window ask to keep its size in backing pixels instead.
- **Windows moves a window back on its own** when a restore finds it completely off the screen, and onto the monitor holding most of it when a monitor is moved. Users on 24H2 report windows moved after a monitor sleeps.
- **A drag fuji runs itself on Windows.** WebView2 keeps the pointer captured outside the window, as a Tauri app resizing its window from the page reports ([WebView2Feedback #5701](https://github.com/MicrosoftEdge/WebView2Feedback/issues/5701)). tao's `set_outer_position` is an asynchronous SetWindowPos, so the window trails the pointer, estimated at a frame or more and not measured.
- **On GNOME and KDE, the drag fuji uses never puts the top above the top bar,** on Wayland or X11. mutter's `constrain_titlebar_visible` holds the top for every move except its own unconstrained ones, the Super drag and the keyboard move, and applies to a window with no title bar too (`constraints.c`). KWin keeps 50 pixels of a window without a title bar on the screen during a restricted move, and its Meta drag is unrestricted. Both keep a strip of the window on the screen at every edge, about 75 pixels on GNOME, and no setting turns it off.
- **On Wayland a window may be bigger than the screen.** mutter takes a client's own size outside fullscreen, and KWin accepts it. On X11 both shrink a window that asks to outgrow the work area. GNOME maximizes a resizable window larger than 80 percent of the work area when it first shows, by its `auto-maximize` setting.
- **Raspberry Pi OS's labwc puts no limit on a move**, only resistance at the edges.
- **A drag fuji runs itself cannot work on Wayland**, where GTK ignores a move of a toplevel window and an application cannot place its own. On X11, mutter and KWin pull a window fuji places back into the work area.

## What the research found about the buttons and the corners

- **The Mac's geometry, measured on the Mac mini with Sequoia 15.7.4.** AppKit makes each button a frame 14 by 16 points. In a standard 28-point title bar, and in a window whose title bar is hidden over full-size content, the circles are 12 points across, centered 14 points down and 14, 34 and 54 points in from the left. A toolbar moves them: centered 19 points in and down in a 38-point compact bar, and 26 in a 52-point unified one. A research session measured this on 2026-10-21 by having AppKit draw its real buttons into memory, with no window shown.
- **The Mac's colors on Sequoia**, from the same measurement, in sRGB. Idle in light: close #FF5F57 with a ring of #E44139, minimize #FEBC2E with #E3A00F, zoom #28C840 with #0AAC22; in dark the same fills with no ring. The hover glyphs in light: × #7E0707, − #B3710C, and the zoom glyph #036804; in dark the × is #A10707. Pressed in light, about three quarters of each fill: #BF4741, #BE8D23, #1E9630. Pressed in dark, lighter instead: #FF8D85, #FFEA5C, #56F66E. Inactive in light: #E5E5E5 with a ring near #C3C3C3. The pointer over any of the three shows the glyphs on all three, which AppKit decides through `_mouseInGroup:`, a private method on the window's frame view.
- **Tahoe and Golden Gate moved them.** Tahoe's are 14-point lights 23 points apart, the first centered 16 points in and down in a 32-point bar, by developers' reports ([bb #5459](https://github.com/get-bb/bb/pull/5459), [IINA's Titlebar.swift](https://github.com/iina/iina/blob/HEAD/iina/Titlebar.swift)), with the layout following the SDK an application is built with, per [Zed #45351](https://github.com/zed-industries/zed/pull/45351). macOS 27, Golden Gate, the current release since mid-September 2026 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/28/apple-releases-macos-27-0-1/)), redesigned them again ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/18/golden-gate-27-window-controls)), and nobody has published their geometry.
- **No ready-made drawing matches the Mac,** and Apple's Human Interface Guidelines say "don't try to replicate the system-provided appearance. Doing so without perfectly matching the system's look and behavior can make your app feel broken." The closest is tauri-controls' SVGs ([macos.tsx](https://github.com/agmmnn/tauri-controls/blob/master/apps/tauri-controls/src/tauri-controls/controls/macos.tsx)). AppKit's `standardWindowButton(_:for:)` makes the running macOS's own buttons for any view, and on a window with no title bar their group hover takes the private `_mouseInGroup:`.
- **Windows draws its buttons from bitmaps, not a font**, a glyph bitmap per DPI in the theme's image atlas ([msstyleEditor's wiki](https://github.com/nptr/msstyleEditor/wiki/Window-Frames)). For an app drawing its own, Microsoft names E921 minimize, E922 maximize, E923 restore and E8BB close ([title bar design](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/basics/titlebar-design)), from Segoe Fluent Icons on Windows 11 and Segoe MDL2 Assets on Windows 10, which Firefox draws its own window buttons with at 10 CSS pixels ([browser.css](https://github.com/mozilla-firefox/firefox/blob/main/browser/themes/windows/browser.css)).
- **Windows' sizes are copied rather than published.** Microsoft gives no pixel sizes for Win32 caption buttons. Firefox, Windows Terminal and tauri-controls make each 46 wide, and Chromium 45 with a 1-pixel gap. Windows 11's DWM makes them 47, 46 and 47 at 100 percent, read from a Windhawk mod reverse-engineered on 25H2. Windows reports a shown window's real button rectangles through `DWMWA_CAPTION_BUTTON_BOUNDS` and `WM_GETTITLEBARINFOEX`.
- **Windows' colors.** Windows 10's close hover is #E81123 with a white glyph and pressed that red at alpha 0x98, and minimize and maximize take the glyph's color at alpha 0x1A on hover and 0x33 pressed, from Chromium's `native_chrome_color_mixer_win.cc`. Windows 11's close hover is #C42B1C and pressed that red at 90 percent, from Windows Terminal's `MinMaxCloseControl.xaml`. No Microsoft document states either red.
- **Linux puts close at the top right everywhere but elementary.** Ubuntu shows minimize, maximize and close on the right; vanilla GNOME on Fedora close alone; KDE Plasma 6, Mint's Cinnamon, Xubuntu and Raspberry Pi OS minimize, maximize and close on the right; elementary close on the left and maximize on the right. GTK's `gtk-decoration-layout` setting carries the user's own order, and fuji's Rust could read it.
- **Ubuntu's own buttons are libadwaita's**: circles 24 CSS pixels across, 13 apart, the outermost centered 23 pixels in from the right edge and down from the top of a 46 or 47 pixel header bar, the circle the text color at 10, 15 and 30 percent, with Yaru's or Adwaita's symbolic SVGs for the glyphs. Electron's Linux buttons have drawn this way since July 2026 ([freedesktop_nav_button_provider.cc](https://github.com/electron/electron/blob/main/shell/browser/ui/views/freedesktop_nav_button_provider.cc)).
- **Mac video players keep the title bar and hide it.** mpv's window is always titled, and `--border=no` hides its title bar with `titlebarAppearsTransparent` and `.fullSizeContentView` (mpv's `video/out/mac/window.swift`). IINA's main window is the same, and Electron's frameless window starts titled. AppKit hands WindowServer a corner mask, and WindowServer clips the window as it composites it ([mac-window-corners](https://github.com/mansarip/mac-window-corners)), so a titled window's corners cost the video nothing inside the application.
- **A borderless window rounds with public API**: the window non-opaque with a clear background, and a layer's `cornerRadius` and `masksToBounds`. Nobody has measured what a non-opaque window costs WindowServer, the radius has to be written down per release, and the shadow follows only after `invalidateShadow`.
- **The radius by release, from reports, with no Apple figure.** Sequoia 10 points. Tahoe 16 for a window with a title bar and no toolbar, 20 with a compact toolbar and 26 with a unified one, where Apple says "Windows with toolbars now use a larger radius… Titlebar-only windows retain a smaller corner radius" (WWDC25 session 310). Golden Gate 20 for every window, by a forum report. No public API reports the radius before macOS 27's `NSView.cornerConfiguration`.
