# Three video window spikes

## The order

- **First, on the Mac: the title bar back and invisible, and the override.** The video window keeps its title bar and draws nothing of it, the overlay style with the title hidden, so the system draws the rounded corners and the real traffic lights over the page. And `TaoWindow` gets a `constrainFrameRect:toScreen:` that leaves the video window wherever it is put. The walk is the corners, the lights and their hover, minimize, zoom, fuji's fullscreen and back, and a drag up past the menu bar, which says whether WindowServer stops the Mac's own drag by itself. Whether the real traffic lights stay is decided by looking at them.
- **Then Windows: a window bigger than the screen, and a drag fuji runs itself past the top**, written on the Mac and walked on the Windows box.
- **Then the buttons on Windows and Linux**, Windows from Microsoft's named glyphs and Linux from libadwaita's measurements with Yaru's icons.
- **Linux's edges are what its desktop allows.**

## Buttons fuji draws

- **The want.** The video window shows the buttons a title bar holds, drawn by fuji in the page at the places, the sizes and the shapes each platform gives its own, so a careful Mac or Windows user finds them familiar and nothing about them slightly off: red, yellow and green on the Mac, minimize, maximize and close on Windows, and the same three on Linux.
- **Nothing more.** Fuji draws those three and none of what the platforms have added around them, like the Snap Layouts flyout Windows 11 shows over maximize or the arrangements the Mac shows over the green button. Those are for setting one document beside another, to read in one and write in the other, more than for a video player, a picture viewer or a file manager.
- **Where the drawings come from.** Fuji takes the buttons from a drawing that already exists and has already been checked against the platform, from an icon library or open-source SVGs drawn to match, rather than drawing its own. A drawing of fuji's own would be unproven, and would stand or fall on a by-eye check, so it is the last resort.
- **Why fuji draws them.** The video window has no title bar, so the page fills it, the user drags it from anywhere, and it can go past the edges of the screen; the platform's buttons went with the title bar. Buttons fuji draws are also a starting point it can change from, such as a background that runs full bleed beneath them and between them.
- **No animation.** Fuji changes as fast as the computer, the way a flip shows the next picture without missing a frame, and the buttons follow the same rule: a hover shows its whole state the instant the pointer arrives, full red rather than a fade into red, even where the platform animates it. What has to be right is each button's shape, position and size; the antialiasing does not have to match the platform's to the pixel.
- **The Mac's geometry, measured on the Mac mini with Sequoia 15.7.4.** AppKit makes each button a frame 14 by 16 points. In a standard 28-point title bar, and in a window whose title bar is hidden over full-size content, the circles are 12 points across, centered 14 points down and 14, 34 and 54 points in from the left. A toolbar moves them: centered 19 points in and down in a 38-point compact bar, and 26 in a 52-point unified one. A research session measured this on 2026-10-21 by having AppKit draw its real buttons into memory, with no window shown.
- **The Mac's colors on Sequoia**, from the same measurement, in sRGB. Idle in light: close #FF5F57 with a ring of #E44139, minimize #FEBC2E with #E3A00F, zoom #28C840 with #0AAC22; in dark the same fills with no ring. The hover glyphs in light: × #7E0707, − #B3710C, and the zoom glyph #036804; in dark the × is #A10707. Pressed in light, about three quarters of each fill: #BF4741, #BE8D23, #1E9630. Pressed in dark, lighter instead: #FF8D85, #FFEA5C, #56F66E. Inactive in light: #E5E5E5 with a ring near #C3C3C3. Dark inactive would not draw offscreen, and whether AppKit color-manages these values on a wide-gamut panel is not known.
- **The Mac's hover is a group.** The pointer over any of the three shows the glyphs on all three, which AppKit decides through `_mouseInGroup:`, a private method on the window's frame view. The zoom glyph is two arrows on a window that can enter a Space, and + on one that cannot, or with Option held.
- **Tahoe moved the Mac's buttons**, by developers' reports and not seen here: 14-point lights 23 points apart, the first centered 16 points in and down in a 32-point bar, as [bb #5459](https://github.com/get-bb/bb/pull/5459) measured on 26.5.2 and [IINA's Titlebar.swift](https://github.com/iina/iina/blob/HEAD/iina/Titlebar.swift) sets. The layout follows the SDK an application is built with: "The size and spacing around the traffic light buttons changes after macOS SDK 26", per [Zed #45351](https://github.com/zed-industries/zed/pull/45351). Fuji builds with SDK 26.2.
- **Golden Gate redesigned them again.** macOS 27, Golden Gate, is the current release since mid-September 2026 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/28/apple-releases-macos-27-0-1/)), so the Mac mini on Sequoia is two releases behind. Its sixth beta changed the traffic lights ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/18/golden-gate-27-window-controls)), and nobody has published their geometry or colors.
- **AppKit hands over the real Mac buttons at runtime.** `standardWindowButton(_:for:)` makes the running macOS's own buttons, which can go in any view: "The caller is responsible for adding the button to the view hierarchy and for setting the target to be the window" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nswindow/standardwindowbutton(_:for:))). On a window with no title bar the group hover takes the private `_mouseInGroup:`, `performClose:` beeps for want of a close button, and minimize needs the miniaturizable style that taking the title bar off removed, as [VLC found](https://mailman.videolan.org/pipermail/vlc-commits/2018-January/045968.html).
- **A Mac window with a hidden title bar keeps the real buttons where they are**, over a page that runs full bleed beneath and between them. Tauri's `TitleBarStyle::Overlay` makes the title bar transparent and the content full size, `hidden_title` hides the title, and `traffic_light_position` moves the buttons, which tao puts back on every draw (tao 0.35.3, `inset_traffic_lights`). That window has a title bar again, which the corners and the edges below bear on.
- **No ready-made drawing matches the Mac.** The closest is tauri-controls' SVGs ([macos.tsx](https://github.com/agmmnn/tauri-controls/blob/master/apps/tauri-controls/src/tauri-controls/controls/macos.tsx)): 12-point circles, fills #ff544d, #ffbd2e and #28c93f, the first centered 18 points in rather than 14, no inactive state and nothing for Tahoe. macos-web and the WhiteSur theme are further off. Apple's Human Interface Guidelines say "don't try to replicate the system-provided appearance. Doing so without perfectly matching the system's look and behavior can make your app feel broken."
- **Windows draws its buttons from bitmaps, not a font.** DWM keeps a glyph bitmap per DPI in the theme's image atlas ([msstyleEditor's wiki](https://github.com/nptr/msstyleEditor/wiki/Window-Frames), [a Windhawk mod's source](https://github.com/ramensoftware/windhawk-mods/blob/main/mods/caption-buttons-customizer.wh.cpp)), which a page cannot reach.
- **Microsoft names the glyphs for buttons an application draws.** "If you create your own caption buttons for your app, follow these guidelines to match the system caption buttons": E921 minimize, E922 maximize, E923 restore and E8BB close ([title bar design](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/basics/titlebar-design)), from Segoe Fluent Icons on Windows 11 and Segoe MDL2 Assets on Windows 10. Windows installs both, and a page names them in CSS; Segoe Fluent Icons "is not included by default on Windows 10". Firefox draws its own window buttons this way at 10 CSS pixels, "Luckily, the relevant glyphs are the same, so we can just fall back" ([browser.css](https://github.com/mozilla-firefox/firefox/blob/main/browser/themes/windows/browser.css)), and tauri-plugin-decorum does inside WebView2. Nobody has compared a 10-pixel glyph with DWM's bitmap at 125, 150 or 175 percent.
- **Windows' sizes are copied rather than published.** Microsoft gives no pixel sizes for Win32 caption buttons. Firefox, Windows Terminal and tauri-controls make each 46 wide, and Chromium 45 with a 1-pixel gap. On the Windows 10 box a title bar is 31 backing pixels at 100 percent, the top 8 of them the resize border, as window.rs measured, and Electron's comments put the buttons almost flush with the window's top right corner, under a one-pixel strip of that border. Windows 11's DWM makes them 47, 46 and 47 at 100 percent, read from a Windhawk mod reverse-engineered on 25H2. Maximized, they shorten to about 23. Windows reports a shown window's real button rectangles through `DWMWA_CAPTION_BUTTON_BOUNDS` and `WM_GETTITLEBARINFOEX`.
- **Windows' colors.** Windows 10's close hover is #E81123 with a white glyph and pressed that red at alpha 0x98, about #F1717C over white, and minimize and maximize take the glyph's color at alpha 0x1A on hover and 0x33 pressed, all from Chromium's `native_chrome_color_mixer_win.cc`, commented "Match the native buttons". Windows 11's close hover is #C42B1C and pressed that red at 90 percent, from Windows Terminal's `MinMaxCloseControl.xaml`, which Microsoft wrote. No Microsoft document states either red, and Chromium, Electron and Firefox still paint #E81123 on Windows 11. An inactive window's glyphs go semi-transparent. With accent color on title bars, `ColorPrevalence` under `HKCU\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Windows\DWM`, the bar takes the accent and the glyph goes black or white by the accent's brightness.
- **Tauri lays its resize band over the top of the Windows buttons.** On an undecorated window with a shadow, tauri-runtime-wry 2.11.4 puts a child window, `TAURI_DRAG_RESIZE_WINDOW`, across the top, `SM_CYFRAME` deep, answering as the top resize border (`undecorated_resizing.rs`). So the top few backing pixels of a button show the resize arrow and never light, where Windows' own leave one pixel.
- **Windows' button behavior.** Maximize shows E923 while the window is maximized. Microsoft says a double click on the title area maximizes and restores, and a right click shows the system menu; Alt+Space already opens it through tao. The buttons are not tab stops.
- **Linux puts close at the top right everywhere but elementary.** Ubuntu shows minimize, maximize and close on the right, `':minimize,maximize,close'` from ubuntu-settings; vanilla GNOME on Fedora shows close alone, the schema's `'appmenu:close'`; KDE Plasma 6, Mint's Cinnamon, Xubuntu and Raspberry Pi OS show minimize, maximize and close on the right; elementary puts close on the left and maximize on the right.
- **Fuji can read the Linux layout from Rust, and the page cannot.** GTK's `gtk-decoration-layout` comes from `org.gnome.desktop.wm.preferences button-layout` on Wayland and from XSettings on X11, and KDE's kde-gtk-config copies KWin's order into it. Chromium and Firefox follow it. A Wayland desktop that sets nothing, such as sway, reports close alone.
- **Ubuntu's buttons are libadwaita's.** Circles 24 CSS pixels across, 13 apart, the outermost centered 23 pixels in from the right edge and down from the top of a 46 or 47 pixel header bar; the circle is the text color at 10, 15 and 30 percent for rest, hover and pressed, the glyph is the text color, and an unfocused window draws them at half opacity (libadwaita's `_header-bar.scss` and `_buttons.scss`). Ubuntu 24.04 ships libadwaita 1.5.0, which fades a hover over 200 ms.
- **The Linux glyphs are open-source SVGs.** Yaru's `window-close-symbolic.svg` and its three siblings, 16 by 16 with 1-pixel strokes, are what Ubuntu shows, and Adwaita's, with 2-pixel strokes, what vanilla GNOME shows. gtk-rs's `IconTheme` finds the user's own theme's file for each name at runtime, which is how Electron's Linux buttons have drawn since July 2026 ([freedesktop_nav_button_provider.cc](https://github.com/electron/electron/blob/main/shell/browser/ui/views/freedesktop_nav_button_provider.cc)), with libadwaita's circles. tauri-controls' GNOME version matches libadwaita's spacing and neither its colors nor its glyphs.
- **Minimize and maximize on GNOME.** mutter minimizes a window that asks even where no minimize button shows. On Wayland a minimized window cannot bring itself back, and the user does it through Activities, Alt+Tab or the dock. Maximized, libadwaita squares the corners and shows the restore glyph.

## Rounded corners on the Mac

- **The want.** The video window on the Mac has rounded corners, as a Mac window with a title bar does. Its corners went square when the title bar came off, since AppKit draws the rounded ones as part of a title bar's frame. Square is fine on Windows and Linux, and Mac users will balk at it.
- **The radius.** Either the system's or one fuji chooses. The system's is moderate on Sequoia and much larger on Tahoe, so matching it means asking the system rather than writing a number down.
- **The questions.** How other Mac applications round a window without a title bar, and how Tauri applications do. Whether rounding makes fuji's code harder or the window slower, and above all whether it costs the video anything once the gray placeholder becomes libmpv drawing into its native view above the page.
- **The bar.** The rounding happens on the Mac alone, only where a window needs it, without making the code much more complex, the window more sluggish, or the video less smooth.
- **Mac video players keep the title bar and hide it.** mpv's window is always titled, closable, miniaturizable and resizable, and `--border=no` hides its title bar with `titlebarAppearsTransparent` and `.fullSizeContentView` (mpv's `video/out/mac/window.swift` and `title_bar.swift`). IINA's main window is the same (`MainWindowController.xib`). Electron's frameless window starts titled, hides the title and the buttons, and calls `setOpaque:NO` (`native_window_mac.mm`). The system draws the corners and the shadow at its own radius, on whichever macOS is running.
- **A titled window's corners cost the video nothing inside the application.** AppKit hands WindowServer a corner mask, and WindowServer clips the window as it composites it, reverse-engineered in [mac-window-corners](https://github.com/mansarip/mac-window-corners). mpv, IINA and QuickTime all play under those corners.
- **A borderless window rounds with public API.** The window goes non-opaque with a clear background, and a layer takes `cornerRadius` and `masksToBounds`, which "creates an implicit clipping mask that matches the bounds of the layer and includes any corner radius effects" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/calayer/maskstobounds)). The web view keeps its own background and is clipped, so nothing touches the private `drawsBackground`; that is reasoned, and nobody has reported it either way. Rounding the web view's own layer leaves fuji's video view, its sibling, outside the mask. Apple's RoundTransparentWindow sample has the shadow follow the shape after `invalidateShadow`, and forum threads report a dark square near the corners and artifacts after opacity changes.
- **What a non-opaque window costs, nobody has measured.** WindowServer blends it over whatever is beneath it rather than covering it. Electron makes every frameless window non-opaque.
- **The radius by release, from reports, with no Apple figure.** Sequoia: 10 points. Tahoe: 16 for a window with a title bar and no toolbar, 20 with a compact toolbar and 26 with a unified one, where Apple says "Windows with toolbars now use a larger radius… Titlebar-only windows retain a smaller corner radius" (WWDC25 session 310). Golden Gate: 20 for every window, by a forum report. No public API reports the radius before macOS 27, whose `NSView.cornerConfiguration` with `containerConcentric` follows the shape of the window a view is in.
- **The private routes.** `_setCornerRadius:` on NSWindow, and overriding `_cornerMask`, which Electron once did and has removed. Tahoe rewrote that code.
- **Tauri's own routes.** Its maintainers advise a transparent title bar rather than `decorations: false` on the Mac ([tauri #4497](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/4497)). `transparent` and window effects need macos-private-api. [Tauri #14165](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/14165) is a web view left square by a radius set on a sibling view.
- **Fuji's fullscreen and the corners.** tao's simple fullscreen takes the title bar's style off, which squares a titled window's corners, and puts the style back a turn later on the main queue, which brings hidden buttons back; Electron hides them again after. A borderless rounded window would have its radius and opacity taken off and put back by `window_fullscreen_enter` and `window_fullscreen_leave`.
- **The video's place in a titled window.** `video_frame` in window.rs measures the page from the window's content layout rectangle, which leaves out a title bar even when the bar is transparent, while WebKit insets the page under a title bar only when the bar is not transparent (`PageClientImplMac.mm`). So the video view would land a title bar's height too low until `video_frame` measures from the content view instead. That is read from the code and not seen.
- **The video never reaches a corner in the window as it stands.** The margin around the screen is a title bar's height, 28 points on the Mac mini, more than any radius above.

## Above the top of the screen, and bigger than it

- **The want.** On the Mac, Windows and Linux, the user drags the video window anywhere, above the menu bar and past every edge, and the window can be taller, wider, or bigger in both than the screen, with no window manager correcting it or moving it back.
- **Why.** Many videos have a raster taller than the screen, or bigger in both directions. A vertical 4K video downloaded from TikTok or the like is 2160 by 3840, and a collector may want it at one raster pixel per panel pixel on a standard-DPI screen 1200 pixels tall, watching the lower part with the top sitting harmlessly above the desktop. Or the screen is 1024 by 768 and the video is 4K in slow motion, at one raster pixel per CSS pixel per backing pixel per panel pixel all the way through. The user reviews it by dragging the window to pan from one part to another, the way the light table pans a picture, but in a window rather than fullscreen, with the menu bar, the dock and the taskbar still there. Every pixel of the video reaches the window even when the window does not all reach the screen. QuickTime, VLC and the others never show such a video's real raster pixels without a screen bigger than the video.
- **The questions.** On each platform, whether this is possible and how hard: how a window gets above the top, taller than the screen, and bigger than it; what fuji has to ask for or turn off to allow it; and how to keep the operating system from taking fright and pulling the window back on screen or cutting it to size.
- **On the Mac, one method keeps both kinds of window below the menu bar, and an application may override it.** Apple's AppKit notes for 10.9: "windows are now constrained to not intersect the menu bar on their containing space. This restriction was already in place for titled windows, but it has been extended to borderless windows whose level is at least NSNormalWindowLevel but less than NSMainMenuWindowLevel. This behavior is implemented in -[NSWindow constraintFrameRect:toScreen:]. You may override that method in an NSWindow subclass to adjust or prevent this constraining." For a titled resizable window the same method also cuts the height to the screen's.
- **Measured on the Mac mini with Sequoia,** with invisible windows a research session made on 2026-10-21. A borderless window at the normal level placed with its top above the menu bar lands with its top at the menu bar's bottom, and placing it hidden only puts that off to the reveal. A window taller than the screen is left where it is once it covers the screen's whole height, so a window 3840 tall can already sit with its top far above the screen. A titled resizable window is cut to the screen's visible height and pulled back on screen when placed far off. With `constrainFrameRect:toScreen:` answering the frame unchanged, a window placed above the menu bar stayed there, through hiding and showing again.
- **tao's window class has no such method, so fuji can add one.** The runtime's `class_addMethod` adds it to `TaoWindow`, which worked on a live window in that measurement. It covers every window of the class, so it decides per window and calls NSWindow's own for the rest. Swapping one window's class instead is ruled out: tao's `sendEvent:` finds its superclass from the window at runtime, and WebKit's key-value observing has already swapped the class. An override that never constrains also turns off AppKit's rescue of a window whose display goes away ([cmux #7084](https://github.com/manaflow-ai/cmux/pull/7084)); Apple's FullScreenWindow sample suspends constraining only while a flag is set.
- **Whether WindowServer stops the Mac's own drag at the menu bar by itself is not known.** AppKit hands the drag to WindowServer and corrects where it ends through `constrainFrameRect:toScreen:`, read in a disassembly of AppKit on Sequoia. One drag with the override in place settles it.
- **Electron and mpv override the same method.** Electron's `enableLargerThanScreen` answers the frame unchanged for a window with no visible title bar, "put the window wherever the developer wanted it to go", and keeps the position constrained for one with a title bar (`electron_ns_window.mm`). mpv's lets its window be any height and keeps the top below the menu bar.
- **A drag fuji runs itself on the Mac.** The page keeps getting the mouse while the button is down outside the window, since dragged events go to the view that took the press. WebKit works out `screenX` from where the window is at that moment, so a window moving under it can feed its own move back. Chromium's tab drag runs in the application instead: a local event monitor, `[NSEvent mouseLocation]`, and `setFrame:display:NO animate:NO` (`window_move_loop.mm`). A move the application makes skips what WindowServer's drag gives, Space switching and tiling among them, and tao's `set_position` always lands a turn later on the main queue.
- **Size on the Mac.** `maxSize` defaults to the largest float. Apple documents limits of 10,000 for a window's size and ±16,000 for its position, and neither was enforced in the measurement. The Mac mini's M2 caps a GL texture and viewport at 16384 backing pixels, under which fall a 2160 by 3840 window at either scale and 8K at one video pixel per CSS pixel on Retina, 15360 by 8640.
- **Several displays on the Mac.** With Displays have separate Spaces on, the default, Apple says "windows may not visibly span displays… A window clips to the edge of the display", so a window bigger than one screen shows only on its own display.
- **On Windows, Windows' own drag keeps the top on the screen.** Users report the title bar snapping back below the top on Windows 10 ([2017](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/2795812/i-actually-want-to-move-a-window-offscreen)), and since Windows 11 24H2, build 26100.2033, a drag that stops where the whole title bar shows. mpv's `w32_common.c` says "Windows itself applies the same constraint during manual move", while "it's not rejected at the Windows API". Whether it holds for a drag begun in a window's client area, as fuji's is, is not yet seen.
- **A window fuji places itself on Windows goes anywhere.** SetWindowPos puts it where it is told, and it skips Snap, which comes with the system's drag.
- **Size on Windows is already free, by the code.** Microsoft: "For a window with the WS_OVERLAPPED or WS_THICKFRAME style, the DefWindowProc function sends the WM_GETMINMAXINFO message to the window. This is done to validate the new size and position", and "By not passing the WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGING message to the DefWindowProc function, an application can override these defaults" ([WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGING](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/winmsg/wm-windowposchanging)). tao answers that message itself and never passes it on, so `window_frame_set` should make a window bigger than the screen as it stands. A border drag past the screen's size takes a maximum size set through Tauri, which tao writes into `ptMaxTrackSize`, and a value too big for a 32-bit integer turns negative there. Direct3D 11 caps a texture at 16384. None of it has run.
- **A window covering the whole monitor on Windows is taken for fullscreen**, and covers the taskbar, unless it carries the window property `NonRudeHWND` before it is shown, per [Raymond Chen in 2025](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/oldnewthing/20250522-00/?p=111211). A window covering less goes under the taskbar.
- **A move to a monitor at another scale grows the window by that scale.** Windows 11 hands over a rectangle scaled for the new DPI, which tao applies as it is, and `WM_GETDPISCALEDSIZE` lets a window ask to keep its size in backing pixels instead.
- **Windows moves a window back on its own** when a restore finds it completely off the screen, and onto the monitor holding most of it when a monitor is moved. Users on 24H2 report windows moved after a monitor sleeps.
- **A drag fuji runs itself on Windows.** WebView2 keeps the pointer captured outside the window, as a Tauri app resizing its window from the page reports ([WebView2Feedback #5701](https://github.com/MicrosoftEdge/WebView2Feedback/issues/5701)). tao's `set_outer_position` is an asynchronous SetWindowPos, so the window trails the pointer, estimated at a frame or more and not measured.
- **On GNOME and KDE, the drag fuji uses never puts the top above the top bar,** on Wayland or X11. mutter's `constrain_titlebar_visible` holds the top for every move except its own unconstrained ones, the Super drag and the keyboard move, and applies to a window with no title bar too (`constraints.c`). KWin keeps 50 pixels of a window without a title bar on the screen during a restricted move, and its Meta drag is unrestricted. Both keep a strip of the window on the screen at every edge, about 75 pixels on GNOME, and no setting turns it off.
- **On Wayland a window may be bigger than the screen.** mutter takes a client's own size outside fullscreen, and KWin accepts it. On X11 both shrink a window that asks to outgrow the work area. GNOME maximizes a resizable window larger than 80 percent of the work area when it first shows, by its `auto-maximize` setting.
- **Raspberry Pi OS's labwc puts no limit on a move**, only resistance at the edges.
- **A drag fuji runs itself cannot work on Wayland**, where GTK ignores a move of a toplevel window. On X11, mutter and KWin pull a window fuji places back into the work area.
